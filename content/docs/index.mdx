Braven runs your staff team. Tickets are the way in — panels members click, threads
your staff work in, transcripts you can search afterwards — but the reason servers
keep it is everything built on top: who was on duty, who answered, who is meeting
their quota, and who has not shown up in three weeks.

## How Braven is organised

Three ideas explain nearly everything:

**Panels open tickets.** A panel is a message with a dropdown or buttons. Each option
is a category, and a category decides where the ticket opens, who can see it, and
whether the member fills in a form first.

**Modules switch features on and off.** Every feature belongs to a module, and
`/modules` turns any of them off for your server. A module you have switched off is
silent — it does not act, and it does not nag.

**Your plan decides what is available.** Free is genuinely usable rather than a demo.
Paid features refuse cleanly and tell you what would unlock them, and downgrading
never deletes anything you have already set up.

## Two things worth knowing early

**Braven never touches your game server.** It reads your FiveM server's public status
endpoints to show player counts, and it receives webhooks from txAdmin and Tebex. It
issues no in-game commands, no RCON, and no in-game bans. Anything that would write
to your server is out of scope on purpose.

**Panels do not expire.** A panel posted a year ago keeps working through every
update, because the buttons carry no hidden state — Braven looks the category up
when somebody clicks. You will not find yourself reposting panels after an update.

<Callout title="Stuck?">
  Every page here lists the exact commands involved. If something is not behaving,
  `/plan` tells you what your server is on and `/modules view` tells you what is
  switched on — between them they explain most surprises.
</Callout>
